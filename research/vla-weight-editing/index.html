Fixing a robot policy by editing 4 attention heads

Abdul Rahim Mirani · November 2026

I ran the π0.5 DROID policy on two manipulation tasks in simulation, found why it fails at sorting blocks, and fixed it by changing its weights directly. No training and no fine-tuning: four attention heads were turned up 3×. Sorting went from 9 to 27 successes out of 30, and a second task the edit was never designed for got better too.

The policy is the public pi05_droid_jointpos checkpoint, used as released. Both tasks run in Isaac Sim with a Franka arm, a Robotiq gripper and DROID's two cameras (one over the shoulder, one on the wrist). The objects are placed slightly differently in every episode.

The process

What I did

  1. 01Run the original modelon both tasks
  2. 02Diagnose Task 1watch the failures, measure what goes wrong
  3. 03Edit the weightsdesigned using Task 1 only
  4. 04Run the edited modelon both tasks, same 30 placements

The edit was designed using only the colour-sorting task. The cups task played no part in it and was only measured at the end. All before and after numbers come from 30 object placements per task that were not used while designing the edit.

Task 1 · Colour sorting

The problem: blocks are carried too low

I expected the model to mix up the colours. It doesn't: when both bins are on the table it puts almost every block in the right one. The real failure is mechanical. The robot carries the block low, the gripper catches the rim of the bin, and the arm drags the whole bin off the table. After that the task can't be finished.

So the fix is simple to state: carry the block higher, without changing how the robot grasps.

Task 1 · Colour sorting

The fix: four “lift higher” dials

π0.5 is built from many small parts called attention heads, 288 of them in total. Each one adds its own small contribution to the robot's next move.

  • 1 · Test each headTurn it off, then double it, and measure one thing: how high the robot carries the block.
  • 2 · Find the dialsFour heads act like a dial. Turn them down and the carry drops; turn them up and it rises. Grasping stays the same.
  • 3 · Turn them upMultiplying all four by 3 raises the carry by 2–3 cm, enough to clear the rim.
Four heads turned upCarry heightGrasp height
×2+1.4 cmno change
×3 (used)+2.3 cmno change
×4+3.0 cmstarts to change

This changes 1.57 million of the model's 3.35 billion weights. The edited model is saved as an ordinary checkpoint and runs with the standard code, with no extra hooks at inference. The tests show what these heads do to the robot's movement; why the model computes it this way internally is a separate question.

Inside π0.5

Where the edit lives

π0.5 is two connected transformers. A large one (2B parameters) reads the camera images, the instruction and the robot's joint positions. A small one (300M, the “action expert”) reads what the large one produced and outputs the next second of movement. Each has 18 layers with 8 heads per layer. Two of the edited heads are in each.

Task 2 · Cups in basket

The same edit also helped the cups task

This task was not used at all while designing the edit; I ran it at the end only to check nothing broke. It improved instead. Cups are carried higher, as with the blocks, and episodes finish sooner. The gain is smaller than on Task 1.

Caveats

Limits

  • One scene per task, in simulation. Other scenes and a real robot are untested.
  • The edited model occasionally puts a blue block in the wrong bin (4 times in 30 episodes), which the original rarely did.
  • The heads were found by testing many candidates against one measurement, not guessed in one shot.
